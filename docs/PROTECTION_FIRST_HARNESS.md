# From recognition to useful protective action

The current evidence shows an uneven capability: models can explain exploitation warning signs and still recommend collecting the disputed debt. Supplying evidence and protective instructions improves some answers, while factual errors and collection assistance remain. Jev handles many explicit screening decisions well, but its arithmetic, ranking consistency and choice of next action need separate checks.

The proposed DueCare extension treats recognition, applicable law and practical protection as separate outputs. A jurisdiction question can remain unresolved while a worker receives useful advice about records, independent support, wages, documents and freedom to leave.

## A clearer evidence path

1. Preserve the complete reported case and the information available at that decision stage. Separate reported claims, verified sources, calculations and missing facts.
2. Map the connected recruitment, training, lending, employment and collection roles. Follow who imposes each cost, receives payment, controls earnings and can impose consequences for refusal or exit.
3. Assess the relevant international labour standards alongside domestic rules. Record the treaty provision, ratification or territorial question, domestic implementation and the source date. State permission and freedom from coercion answer different questions.
4. Select concrete, proportionate protective steps. Explain their relevance and safe preconditions. In an ambiguous case, verification can accompany worker protection; in a benign case, ordinary explanation and routine record checking may be enough.
5. Check the proposed answer for invented authorities, incorrect amounts, unsupported criminal conclusions, collection assistance, coercive implications and contradictions between a warning and the recommended action.
6. Preserve uncertainty and route consequential unresolved questions to qualified review. Record which parts the system answered, which it could not establish and what evidence would change the assessment.

This is a proposed, testable improvement to the research harness. Its benefit must be measured on matched inputs and held-out cases, including examples where concern is unsupported.

## ILO principles and legal scope

The ILO's explanation of Convention 29 connects forced labour to work or service, menace of a penalty and involuntariness. Informed consent and practical freedom to leave matter. The definition does not depend on crossing a border. [ILO definition](https://www.ilo.org/topics/forced-labour-modern-slavery-and-trafficking-persons/what-forced-labour).

The fair-recruitment principles place recruitment fees and related costs away from workers. That supplies an international benchmark for examining the actual cost burden, including an arrangement described as reimbursement or an interest-free plan. [ILO fair-recruitment guidance](https://www.ilo.org/global/topics/labour-migration/publications/WCMS_536755/lang--en/index.htm).

Convention 181 Article 7 addresses agency fees and contains defined competent-authority exceptions. A signature alone does not establish such an exception. Convention 95 addresses freedom to dispose of wages, permitted deduction conditions, and deductions paying employers or intermediaries for access to or retention of employment. Treaty scope and domestic implementation need their own examination. [Convention 181](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB:12100:0::NO::P12100_ILO_CODE:C181), [Convention 95](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB:12100:0::NO::P12100_ILO_CODE:C095).

Ratification accepts a binding international obligation; a domestic implementation gap does not erase it. The ILO Declaration also commits Members to fundamental principles, including elimination of forced labour, even where they have not ratified the relevant Convention. The harness should distinguish these obligations from a claim that every country is bound by every provision of every Convention. [Ratification and implementation](https://www.ilo.org/international-labour-standards/how-international-labour-standards-are-created), [fundamental-principles follow-up](https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/annual-review-under-follow-ilo-declaration-fundamental-principles-and).

## Test the explanation before adopting it

The earlier Jev menu included “check applicable jurisdictions and financing terms,” which became its leading choice in three of four original advice cases. That observation leaves several explanations open: the available options, legal uncertainty in the case, weak use of labour standards, or a tendency to defer practical action.

The extension tests these separately:

- Keep each complete case fixed and compare the original menu, a menu without the generic jurisdiction-check option, and a menu with more concrete protective actions.
- Retain a genuine insufficient-information response and an ordinary-explanation option for benign controls.
- Ask balanced true/false questions about the standards, with and without supplied source summaries.
- Hold an asserted approval claim fixed while varying whether the prompt foregrounds domestic approval or international labour protection.
- Separate source context from harness instructions. The earlier evidence-assisted condition changed both at once.

Removing an answer option changes the decision problem. A more protective choice after removal is evidence of menu sensitivity; demonstrating knowledge of a standard requires a separate test.

## Improvements beyond a prompt wrapper

The harness can support reliable source retrieval, explicit calculations, evidence tracking and a check of the proposed action. Model-side improvement also needs specialist and worker-informed examples, contrastive benign cases, correction of legal hallucinations, multilingual review and repeated testing across pinned model versions. A larger roster increases coverage; matched questions and transparent scoring make that coverage interpretable.

The report should distinguish three claims: how the current configurations differ, what changes under guidance, and what improves over time. Each needs its own matched evidence. The current study supports the first two on its selected cases. Longitudinal improvement and the proposed ILO-specific explanation remain questions for the expanded experiments.
