# Presentation variation protocol

Quality, register, format, length and specificity are separate design dimensions. A terse answer can be excellent; a fluent, technical answer can be wrong. Colloquial answers receive the same substantive quality assessment as other registers. Specific details must come from the supplied facts.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Requested quality | worst, bad, neutral, good, best |
| Length | 25-55, 56-95, 96-175, 280-450 words |
| Format | prose, bullets, memo, explained screening, Q&A, table, JSON, assessor-reviewer dialogue |
| Register | very simple, plain, colloquial, professional, technical, dense specialist |
| Specificity | general but supported, linked to case facts, explicit evidence distinctions |
| Prose | compact, flowing, varied sentence length |

The supplement contains 72 concern, control and information-gap cases across 24 mechanism families. Each case has 12 presentation profiles: four lengths and three variants. Every register appears twice per case, and all eight formats occur. The same 12 profiles are reused for all five requested quality tiers: 72 x 12 x 5 = 4,320 Tactical requests. The ordinary best-effort baseline for each comparison model contains 864 requests.

The design samples combinations of the dimensions. Presentation assignments are reproducible. Tactical uses sampling temperatures of 0.35, 0.65 and 0.95 through a hosted adapter with service-controlled randomness. Cloud baselines use their declared fixed decoding settings. Historic source examples retain their original text and protocol.

The public version 2 comparison fixture contains 1,728 requests: 864 expected ties and 864 altered-conclusion controls, including both candidate positions. Judges receive the generic screening policy and the two candidates. Expected outcomes and equivalence labels stay in the local references. Position stability and reference agreement are reported separately. Always answering "tie" scores 50% on this mixed fixture.

An earlier private version explicitly asserted pair equivalence. It remains a labelled, cued diagnostic. The current blind comparison has its own protocol, and candidate-generation runs retain the inputs they actually used. References express expectations under the constructed screening policy. Independent human adjudication is a further validation step.

The original 20,080-candidate campaign retains its versioned design: 251 source prompts, 248 distinct bounded fact summaries, four length bands and four variants per requested tier and length. Its eight-style pool has its own assignments. The supplement adds explicit register and specificity dimensions. Analyses of both studies retain the protocol ID for every observation.

Low-tier examples use bounded abstract errors or omissions. Candidate-generation controls restrict operationally enabling detail. Judges receive the case and answer with requested tiers and style assignments kept in local metadata. Reports track refusals, tier mismatches, duplicates and length misses alongside usable and assessed responses.
